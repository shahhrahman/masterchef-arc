# Approaches

How recipes must be written, and the named cooking modes to choose from. When asking for a recipe, name one approach. If none is named, assume **Hands-off batch**.

## Universal rules (apply to every approach)

1. **Measure everything.** Salt, spices, oil, water, rice, protein, vegetables. No "to taste", "a pinch", "a splash", or "until it looks right". Use tsp, tbsp, cups, and package units (lb, oz, cans). See `kitchen.md` for available tools.
2. **Every step is self-contained.** Each step restates the exact quantity, temperature, pressure setting, and time it needs. The cook should never have to look back at the ingredient list to execute a step.
3. **Appliance settings are explicit.** For the Instant Pot: mode (Sauté / Pressure Cook), pressure level, minutes, and release method with its duration. For the air fryer: °F and minutes. For the rice cooker: water-to-rice ratio and which cup measure.
4. **Only use what is in `pantry.md`.** If a dish really needs something missing, say so before the recipe and offer the closest in-stock substitute.
5. **State the yield.** Say how many meals the recipe makes and the approximate portion size per meal.
6. **Keep the ingredient list anyway.** It is useful for prep and shopping, but it is not a substitute for rule 2.
7. **Pre-mix the spices.** If two or more ground spices, salt, or other dry seasonings go into the pot at the same step, do not list them one by one inside that step. Instead, add a "Spice mix" prep step before any heat is on: measure every spice into one small bowl, with exact amounts, and stir. Then the cooking step says "add the spice mix" and restates its full contents in brackets. Reason: with Sauté on, there is no time to measure five jars one at a time; things burn and stick.
8. **Minimise prep bowls.** When several ingredients are chopped or measured during prep, group everything that goes into the pot at the same moment into one bowl, and say so explicitly ("chop A, B, and C into the same bowl"). Only ask for a separate bowl when the ingredient truly must go in at a different time, and say why in one phrase. Never instruct "add A" later if A was stacked under B and C in a shared bowl. The target is the fewest vessels possible during prep: one bowl per "add to pot" moment.
9. **No fishing, no re-pouring.** Never ask the cook to pick pieces out of the pot, or to ladle liquid out to measure it and pour it back. If a stage needs a known amount of liquid, measure it on the way in. When two components need different cooking (rice and a wet meat stage, say), give each its own appliance and combine at the end.

Rules 7 and 8 together mean every recipe has a short **Prep** section before the first cooking step, listing each bowl and exactly what is in it.

## Named approaches

### Hands-off batch

- **Goal:** cook once, eat for 3 days (6 meals: lunch and dinner).
- **Pot:** the 8 qt Instant Pot, plus the rice cooker when the meal has plain rice (see `kitchen.md`). No second pan, no separate stovetop step.
- **Effort:** minimal babysitting. Load, set, walk away. A short Sauté step at the start is acceptable; constant stirring is not.
- **Time:** prefer under 15 minutes of hands-on time. Total time is less important than hands-on time.
- **Output:** a recipe that stores and reheats well across 3 days. Mention how to store and reheat.
- **Scaling:** size everything for 6 portions. Respect the Instant Pot max-fill line for the 8 qt model.

### Weekend project

- **Goal:** something that tastes noticeably better than a Hands-off batch, for a weekend or any day with free time. Flavor comes first. Extra effort is welcome when it buys flavor (browning, marinating, cooking in stages), not for its own sake.
- **Pot / appliance:** Instant Pot first, in stages when the dish benefits: Sauté onions until browned, pressure cook, then reduce or finish on Sauté. The air fryer, rice cooker, and microwave may each take a step alongside it (for example crisp the protein in the air fryer while the rice cooker makes rice). Stovetop and oven stay opt-in per `kitchen.md`.
- **Effort:** moderate, and every bit of it spelled out. Chopping, marinating, browning in batches, a second cooking stage, and a finishing step are all fine. A stirred stage is fine if it is under 10 minutes and the step says how long. Still a home cook's dish: no deep frying, no dough from scratch, no technique that needs practice to get right.
- **Time:** up to about 45 minutes hands-on. A marinade may start hours ahead or the night before; say so at the top, with the minimum useful marinating time. Total time does not matter.
- **Output:** the universal rules still apply: exact measurements, self-contained steps, a spice mix per stage, one bowl per add-to-pot moment (more bowls than usual is fine when there are more stages). Say how it stores and reheats. If a part is best fresh (a crisped topping, a garnish), say which part and how to redo it on days 2 and 3.
- **Scaling:** 6 portions, same as Hands-off batch, unless the request says otherwise. Respect the Instant Pot max-fill line for the 8 qt model.
